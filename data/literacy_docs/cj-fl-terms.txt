General Market Terms
SPY / S&P 500: A major stock market index that tracks the 500 largest companies in the U.S. Often used as the benchmark for performance.

Alpha: Extra return generated compared to the benchmark (SPY). Positive = outperformance.

Beta: Measure of volatility vs the market. Beta 1.0 = moves like SPY; >1 = more volatile; <1 = more stable.

Volatility (σ / Standard Deviation): A statistical measure of how much prices move around. Used to define “risk bands” like 1σ, 2σ, 3σ.

Gross Exposure: Total size of all positions (long + short), compared to portfolio value.

Net Exposure: Long positions minus short positions (net market exposure).

Drawdown: The percentage decline from the portfolio’s peak to its lowest point.

Earnings & Valuation Metrics (Zacks/FactSet style)
EPS (Earnings Per Share): A company’s profit divided by shares outstanding. EPS0 = last quarter; EPS1/2 = future quarters.

EG1 / EG2 (Earnings Growth): Growth rate of EPS. EG1 = near-term; EG2 = further forward.

P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio): Current price divided by earnings. High P/E = expensive, low P/E = cheap.

PEG (Price/Earnings-to-Growth): P/E ratio divided by earnings growth. PEG < 1 = undervalued, PEG > 2 = often overvalued.

Market Cap: Total company value = price × shares outstanding.

Optionable: Whether options contracts are available for the stock.

PTM Cheat Sheet Strategy Terms
PTM (Play the Macro): Your strategy framework; combine macroeconomic signals with stock-level factors.

Positive → More Positive: The ideal setup — earnings growth improves, valuation remains attractive.

Distribution Phase: When hedge funds quietly sell into strength, often while media is hyping.

Stagflation: Macro regime where growth is weak but inflation is high (defensive sectors win).

Contraction Phase: Recessionary setup, defensive play is preferred.

News & Sentiment
Catalyst: An event or news trigger that can move a stock (earnings, regulation, product launch).

Fear/Greed Sentiment: When news gets extremely negative → often a contrarian buy signal; when extremely positive → often a signal insiders are selling.

Fake News / Noise: News not relevant to earnings, cash flows, or macro trends — must be filtered out.

Macro Indicators
Yield Curve: The spread between short-term and long-term interest rates. Inversion = recession signal.

M2 Money Supply: Total money in circulation (cash, deposits) — important for liquidity.

Credit Spreads (IG, HY): Difference in yield between safe bonds (investment grade) and risky ones (junk bonds). Wider spreads = more stress.

ISM PMI: Monthly survey of purchasing managers. >50 = expansion, <50 = contraction.

VIX (Volatility Index): “Fear gauge” for the market, higher = more volatility expected.

Building Permits: Leading indicator for housing and economic growth.

Commodity Prices: Oil, copper, lumber, etc. Used to gauge inflation and economic activity.

Portfolio & Risk Management
TWR (Time-Weighted Return): Portfolio return metric that removes the effect of adding/removing cash. Best for comparing to benchmarks.

PRM (Pre-Emptive Risk Management): Risk rules applied before trades (e.g., don’t exceed exposure/beta limits).

RRM (Reactive Risk Management): Adjustments made during trades (e.g., cutting losers, adding to winners).

Guardrails: Limits like gross exposure >200%, net <50%, beta >1.3 — warnings when breached.

Attribution Table: Breakdown showing how each stock contributed to overall portfolio return and risk.

Technical Analysis (Charts & Timing)
Standard Deviation Bands (σ bands): Expected move ranges — 1σ (68%), 2σ (95%), 3σ (99%). Used for price alerts.

Percentile Probabilities: Probability levels in 5% increments (e.g., “10% chance stock drops -15% this month”).

Fibonacci (optional): A sequence-based tool for timing. In simplified mode, replaced by σ/percentiles here.

OHLCV Data: Open, High, Low, Close, Volume — raw daily trading data.
